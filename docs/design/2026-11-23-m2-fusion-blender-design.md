# M2: Fusion 360 Design + Blender Showcase

- **Status:** approved by the owner (2026-11-24)
- **Date:** 2026-11-23
- **Parent spec:** `docs/design/2026-11-23-rocky-walker-design.md` (§5.2 was revised to Fusion 360 on this date)
- **Scope:** the complete robot designed in Fusion 360, then shown assembled, walking and dancing in Blender, animated by the real motion code. There's no physical hardware in this milestone. The bench leg (servo driver, calibration, real servos) becomes the next milestone.

## 1. Intent

The owner wants **one central, precise design** of Rocky in Fusion 360, built through the Fusion MCP connection so they can watch and adjust it live. That design then goes into **Blender** to show what the finished robot looks like assembled, walking in any direction, turning, and doing dance moves.

The showcase must be honest. **Every joint angle in the animation comes from the same `MotionController` and gait code that will drive the real servos**, not from hand animation. If it walks well in Blender, the code does what we think it does.

## 2. Goals

1. **A complete Rocky in Fusion 360:**
   - body: chassis + faceted, rock-like carapace
   - 5 identical legs, each with a coxa bracket, femur and tibia
   - MG996R servo pockets, idler bearings, metal horns, and M3 screws with captive nuts
   - mounts for the Pi 5, PCA9685, battery, regulators and Camera Module 3
2. **Parametric and in sync with the code.** Key dimensions are Fusion **user parameters** with the same names and values as `config/rocky.toml` (lengths, mount radius, servo size, clearance). Changing a parameter in Fusion and the matching TOML value keeps CAD and code in agreement.
   - **When physical fit and the TOML disagree, the physical design wins** *(Claude's call)*. Some M1 values were never checked against real parts. For example, `body.thickness_mm = 40` is only the sim's collision cylinder, a vertical MG996R is about 43 mm tall, and `tibia_deg` down to −150° may not clear a knee servo.
   - The CAD may revise body geometry (`mount_radius_mm`, `thickness_mm`) and **joint limits** to what the parts physically allow. Each change is written back to the TOML, followed by a full test run (including the controller's walking-envelope check) and the torque sweep, and noted in build log #2.
   - Leg lengths (40/70/100) change only if that re-check fails, following the parent spec's give-way order.
3. **Posable in Fusion.** The 15 joints are Fusion revolute joints with the config's limits, so the owner can drag a leg around in Fusion.
4. **Real mass numbers.** Fusion's physical properties replace the M1 mass estimates, and the M1 torque sweep is re-run. If the rule fails, the parent spec's "shrink before upgrading" order applies. *(Claude's calls:)*
   - **Printed parts** use an effective density of **0.6 × solid PLA** (1.24 g/cm³ solid). That's a conservative stand-in for 4 walls and 30–40% infill: it overestimates, which is the safe direction.
   - **Bought parts** use their datasheet masses: MG996R 55 g, Pi 5 46 g, and so on.
   - **The sim gets mass *and* centre of mass per segment** (torso, coxa, femur, tibia). The config gains a COM offset along each segment. The direct-drive knee servo puts the femur's COM far from the middle, and that's exactly the load the torque rule tests. `rocky/sim/model.py` uses these instead of uniform capsules. Inertia stays approximated from the capsule. Meshes in MuJoCo are visual only and optional.
5. **Published files**, committed in `cad/` *(Claude's call)*. This replaces the parent spec's "STLs on releases" rule, which assumed STLs could be regenerated from code. With Fusion they can't, so they are the hardware deliverable.
   - `rocky.f3d` (editable source)
   - `rocky.step` (any CAD tool)
   - `stl/` (one STL per printable part, already oriented for printing)
   - `meshes/` (one mesh per moving segment, for Blender)

   Rendered **MP4s go on GitHub releases**. **GIFs** and the hero still are committed under `docs/build-log/media/`.
6. **Blender showcase**, generated by a script run headless from this repo:
   - the assembled robot with the blue/white/black PLA colours, a floor, lighting and a camera
   - clips: walk forward, walk diagonally, turn in place, and a **dance routine**
   - MP4s plus GIFs and a hero still for the README and build log #2
7. **Dance moves as data.** Moves are keyframe files in `moves/` (body sway, bob, twist, tilt, a leg wave), played by a new `rocky.moves` player through the same safety guards as walking (joint limits, servo speed). The real robot will later play the same files.

## 3. Non-goals

- Real servos, PCA9685 code, calibration tool, power-up sequence (next milestone)
- Wiring and cable routing detail in CAD (mounting points only)
- Physics in Blender. Blender is for looks; MuJoCo remains the strength check.
- Photorealistic rendering. A clean, readable look is enough (EEVEE).
- A hand-sculpted carapace. Faceted is the style.

## 4. Anti-goals

- **Hand-animated motion in Blender.** If the animation doesn't come from the motion code, the showcase proves nothing.
- **Feet sliding in the animation.** Stance feet must stay planted on the floor. A sliding foot means the body motion and the gait disagree.
- **CAD that doesn't match the code:** leg lengths, joint axes or mount positions that differ from `config/rocky.toml`.
- **Parts that can't be printed** on a 180 mm bed, or that need supports everywhere.
- **A design only the owner can open.** STEP and STL are always published next to the `.f3d`.

## 5. Approach

The owner set the approach: Fusion 360 as the central design, Blender for the showcase. This milestone records the details.

### 5.1 How the Fusion design gets built

- Claude builds the design **through the Fusion MCP** by running Fusion API Python scripts in the owner's open document. The owner watches live and can edit anything by hand. The MCP's `script` operation, which runs arbitrary Fusion API Python and returns its output, was verified working on 2026-11-23.
- **The `.f3d` is the source of truth** *(Claude's call, following the owner's "central design")*. The build scripts are saved in `cad/fusion/` as a readable record of how each feature was made. They are one-off, not a guaranteed rebuild, so hand edits by the owner don't need to be folded back into them.
- **Component structure** (one Fusion component per printable or bought part):
  - `Body`: chassis plates, carapace wedges, electronics mounts
  - `Leg`: coxa bracket, femur, tibia, plus servo and bearing placeholders. Built **once** and placed 5 times at 72° steps around the body.
  - Bought-part placeholders (MG996R, horn, 623-size bearing, Pi 5, PCA9685, LiPo, UBEC, camera), with real masses, so mass and fit are realistic. They are excluded from the STLs.
- **Joint geometry matches the code exactly** (parent spec §6 and `rocky/kinematics`):
  - the coxa axis is vertical, at the mount radius from the body centre
  - the femur axis is horizontal, `coxa` mm out from the coxa axis
  - the tibia (knee) axis is parallel to the femur axis, `femur` mm further out
  - the foot tip is `tibia` mm from the knee
  - the coxa, femur and knee axes are all at the same height, so the legs' zero pose is straight out horizontally
- **Knee servo: direct drive, mounted on the femur at the knee** (Claude's call; the owner didn't choose). It is simple, has no play, matches the M1 sim's mass assumption, and is the standard hobby-hexapod layout.
- **Coxa servos** sit inside the chassis, vertical, one per leg at the mount radius. **Femur servos** ride on the coxa brackets.
- **Servo fit** comes from parameters (`servo_body_w/l/h`, ear-hole spacing, shaft offset, `fit_clearance`), set from the MG996R datasheet. They are confirmed with calipers once servos arrive. A small **fit coupon** part (one servo pocket, one bearing seat, one captive-nut slot) is included so a rebuilder can test-print tolerances first.

### 5.2 How the Blender showcase gets built

- **Meshes:** one mesh per moving segment (torso, and the coxa, femur and tibia of each leg), exported from Fusion with each segment's origin on its joint axis. This export convention is the bridge between the two tools.
- **Scene script** (`showcase/build_scene.py`, run headless: `Blender --background --python ...`): imports the meshes and arranges them in a parent–child hierarchy along each leg's kinematic chain (torso → coxa → femur → tibia), with each pivot on its joint axis. It then adds materials, floor, lights and a camera.
- **Animation:**
  1. A Python step in this repo's environment runs the `MotionController` (walking) or the `rocky.moves` player (dances) at the video frame rate.
  2. It writes each frame's 15 joint angles **plus the body's world pose** to a JSON file.
  3. The Blender script keyframes those values onto the hierarchy.
- **Body motion while walking is kinematic, not physics.** The body moves through the world at exactly the commanded velocity. Stance feet move at minus that velocity in the body frame, so in the world they stay still. Feet stay planted by construction, and the export checks it: stance feet drift under 1 mm.
- **Rendering:** EEVEE at 1280 × 720, 30 fps, MP4. GIFs at reduced size for the README.

### 5.3 Dance moves (new software)

- `rocky/moves/`: a move is a list of keyframes, each with a time, a **body pose** (x, y, z offset; roll, pitch, yaw) and optional **per-leg foot offsets** (to lift or wave a foot). Between keyframes, poses are eased.
- The player computes the foot targets in the body frame (planted feet stay put in the world while the body moves above them), runs IK, then applies the same joint-limit and servo-speed guards as the walking controller. Every guard that engages is counted, and tests assert a valid move needs none.
- **Moves are TOML files** in `moves/`: `sway`, `bob`, `twist`, `tilt-circle`, `leg-wave`, and a `routine` that chains them. Each one is validated at load: every pose must be reachable and within joint limits.
- **Moves must be physically safe, not just reachable** *(Claude's call: the real robot will play the same files)*:
  - **Static stability:** at every sampled instant, the centre of mass (from the config masses) projects inside the polygon of planted feet, with at least 15 mm margin.
  - **Torque:** the routine is played in MuJoCo and must meet the torque rule.
  - A move that fails either check is rejected by the tests, not toned down by hand.

## 7. Decisions

**By the owner:**
- Fusion 360 is the central design, built through the Fusion MCP.
- Blender shows the assembled robot walking and dancing.

**Claude's calls (the owner can override):**
- Knee servo direct drive on the femur.
- Physical fit wins over TOML values; changes are written back and re-checked.
- Printed-part mass is 0.6 × solid PLA; the sim gets per-segment mass and COM.
- The `.f3d` is the source of truth; build scripts in `cad/fusion/` are a record.
- CAD files committed; MP4s on releases; GIFs committed.
- Dance moves pass static-stability and torque checks.
- Hierarchy instead of an armature in Blender.
- Kinematic body motion in the animation.
- EEVEE rendering.
- Dance moves as TOML keyframe files.
- No hardware in this milestone; the bench leg moves to the next one.
